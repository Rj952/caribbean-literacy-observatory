    About

    Founding statement

    The Caribbean Literacy Observatory is a research-led initiative established to advance the understanding, development and transformation of literacy across the Caribbean.

    Recognising literacy as a multidimensional, dynamic and lifelong process, the Observatory seeks to bring together educational research, statistical evidence, theoretical perspectives, policy analysis and technological innovation within a Caribbean-centred intellectual framework.

    Its purpose is to strengthen regional knowledge production, increase the visibility of Caribbean literacy scholarship, identify research gaps and support evidence-informed educational practices and policies.

    The Observatory embraces linguistic diversity, cultural identity, educational equity, inclusion and the evolving nature of literacy in an increasingly digital and AI-mediated society.

    Through research, collaboration and responsible technological innovation, the Observatory seeks to contribute to the development of more equitable and literate Caribbean societies.

    Founder: Dr. Rohan Jowallah

    Open data commitment

    The Observatory works only with publicly available datasets and publicly accessible research. It does not collect student records, confidential institutional data or personally identifiable information. Its methods are secondary data analysis, evidence synthesis and documentary research.

    • Publicly accessible without special institutional permission
    • Published by an identifiable, credible source, with verifiable attribution
    • Licensed for the intended analysis, reuse and dissemination
    • Accompanied by indicator definitions and methodological information
    • Free from personal or confidential information, and independently verifiable

    Public access does not by itself grant the right to redistribute. Each dataset's licence is checked and recorded before reuse.

      Data status

      Gaps are findings

      Where a figure is missing, old or not comparable, the Observatory says so. It never fills a gap with an invented, interpolated or extrapolated number. Estimates published by official sources are labelled as estimates.

      What literacy rates measure

      The youth and adult literacy rates in the international series are usually based on census or household survey questions, often self- or household-reported, and record literacy as a yes-or-no state. They say little about depth of reading, and nothing about literacy in Caribbean Creole languages. Examining that limit is part of the research agenda.

        Data integrity principles

        1. Retrieve data only from credible, publicly accessible sources.
        2. Prioritise official national, regional and international repositories.
        3. Exclude confidential records, private student information and personal data.
        4. Verify the licence and permitted use of every dataset.
        5. Preserve original datasets and document every transformation.
        6. Record source URL, publisher, reference year, publication date, retrieval date and method.
        7. Separate published statistics from the Observatory's own calculations and interpretations.
        8. Mark missing, outdated, incomplete and non-comparable data.
        9. Never fabricate, interpolate or extrapolate missing statistics.
        10. Never show synthetic or demonstration data on the public platform.
        11. Make every visualisation traceable to its source.
        12. Let users reach original datasets and reproduce findings, within licence terms.
        13. Separate direct literacy measures from proxies such as examination results.
        14. Do not present correlation as causation.
        15. Check every substantive interpretation against the evidence before publishing it.
        16. Keep a transparent data catalogue, version history and correction log.

        Artificial intelligence may help retrieve, organise, validate, analyse and visualise data. It is never the source of a statistic. The Observatory's credibility comes before the volume of data or the sophistication of its technology.

                Accessibility statement

                Built to be read by everyone

                The Observatory aims to meet the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2 at level AA.

                What you can adjust

                Choose Reading options at the top of any page to set:

                • Text size — standard, large or larger. All text is sized so it also follows your browser's own text-size setting.
                • Contrast — a high-contrast mode in black, white and deep blue that meets the stricter AAA level (at least 7:1). It switches on automatically when your system asks for more contrast.
                • Font — expressive headings, plain headings in the same font as the body, or Lexend, a typeface designed to ease reading.
                • Spacing — wider line, letter, word and paragraph spacing.
                • Motion — follow your system setting or stop all animation.
                • Links — underline every link, including buttons and menus.

                How the design handles colour and type

                • Body text uses Atkinson Hyperlegible, a typeface designed for readers with low vision, at 17px, with lines kept under about 70 characters.
                • No text is smaller than 14px. Long passages are not set in italics.
                • Colour never carries meaning alone: data status is shown by marker shape and a written label; female and male values are a diamond and a circle; outdated bars are also striped.
                • Every chart has a text equivalent: the values are written on the bars, listed in tables, and available as CSV.
                • Pages work with a keyboard alone, show a visible focus ring on every background, and move focus to the page heading when you change section.
                • The site respects light and dark modes.

                Known limitations

                • These checks were made by the Observatory's own review on 27 November 2026: measured colour contrast, keyboard paths and layout at phone and desktop widths. The site has not yet been independently audited or tested with screen-reader users; that is planned before public launch.
                • The overview star chart is decorative and approximate in position; its information is repeated in text.
                • Linked sources, such as the World Bank pages, are outside the Observatory's control.
                • The content editor at /admin is third-party software and has its own accessibility record.

                Tell us about a barrier

                If anything stops you from using the site, report an accessibility barrier. We will log it and tell you what we changed.

                Measured colour contrast

                WCAG AA needs 4.5:1 for text and 3:1 for borders, chart marks and focus rings.
